Repiping in Grapevine when spot repairs stop making sense

Repiping is the replacement of a house's water supply lines, either part of the house or all of it. Repiping in Grapevine is worth considering when you have had more than one leak in a short time, when water comes out discolored or when pressure is low throughout the house. The 24/7 line at (817) 601-6266 also takes planning calls, and same-day appointments are offered when the schedule allows.

A repipe is a planned project, not an emergency. If a pipe is bursting now, see burst pipe repair first. A plumber can stop the water and fix the immediate failure, and then talk through whether a repipe makes sense.

This page explains the signs, the materials and what the project involves.

Signs it may be time to repipe

No single sign decides it. A pattern does.

More than one leak in a short time

When one pinhole opens, others in the same pipe are likely to follow. Repair after repair adds up.

Discolored or rusty water

Brown or orange water from the tap, especially in the morning, suggests corroding steel pipe.

Low pressure throughout

Pipes that are scaled or corroded inside restrict flow. USGS notes that scale can narrow pipes and reduce pressure.

Known problem pipe material

Galvanized steel, polybutylene and some older fittings have a track record of problems.

Water that tastes metallic

Corroding pipe can affect taste.

What a repipe involves

A whole-house repipe takes planning and is done in stages to limit disruption.

1

Inspect and plan

The plumber maps the existing lines and chooses the new routing.

2

Open and run

Small access holes are made, and new lines are run.

3

Connect and test

Fixtures are reconnected, and the system is pressure-tested.

4

Patch and finish

Access holes are patched. Painting is separate.

Permits and inspections in Grapevine

The City of Grapevine lists the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), effective January 1, 2024, with its own plumbing amendment. A repipe changes the plumbing system throughout the house, so ask Building Services at (817) 410-3165 which permit and inspections apply before the work begins.

A permitted repipe is inspected, which protects you at resale and with your insurer. To check any plumber's license, use the Public License Search at the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners.

Water quality and the new pipes

Grapevine's 2024 water quality report lists chloramines as the disinfectant and 117 to 135 ppm hardness. New pipes do not change the water, but they remove the old pipe's corrosion and scale from the path to the tap.

If you are repiping and also thinking about treating the water, doing both at once is easier than going back later. See water softener and filtration.

What to do if a pipe fails before the repipe

A failing old pipe can leak before you are ready to replace it.

  • Shut off the main valve to stop the water.
  • Call the 24/7 line for a plumber to isolate the failed section.
  • Photograph the damage for your insurer.
  • Ask the plumber to note the pipe material and condition, which helps plan the repipe.

Questions about repiping in Grapevine

How do I know whether I need a repipe or just repairs?

A single leak in an otherwise sound line is a repair. A pattern of leaks, discolored water or low pressure throughout points to a repipe.

How long does a repipe take?

Many houses take a few days. Access, the size of the house and finishing work change that, and the plumber gives you the time with the price.

Is PEX or copper better?

Each works well. PEX is flexible and quick to install, and copper has a long record. Your plumber can recommend based on your house.

Do I need a permit to repipe?

Probably, but permit rules vary. Ask Grapevine Building Services at (817) 410-3165 before the work starts.

Will I have water during the repipe?

Usually water is shut off during the day and restored at night. The plumber explains the schedule.
