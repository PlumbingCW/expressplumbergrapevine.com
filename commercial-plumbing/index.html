Business emergency

When a commercial plumbing problem cannot wait

Any of these can close a business for the day:

  • Sewage backing up into floor drains or restrooms.
  • A burst or leaking supply line flooding the space.
  • No hot water in a kitchen or a restroom.
  • A grease line that is backed up and overflowing.
  • A water main or meter leak outside the building.

Grease traps and kitchen drains

A restaurant's grease trap or interceptor catches fats, oils and grease before they enter the sewer. If it is not cleaned on schedule, grease reaches the building's drain line and hardens, and the kitchen backs up at the worst time. Cleaning frequency depends on volume, so follow the schedule that your trap size and local rules require.

Hydro jetting clears hardened grease from the pipe wall in a way a snake cannot. A camera inspection afterward shows whether the pipe is clean or damaged.

Backflow testing

A backflow preventer is a valve assembly that keeps water from flowing backward into the public supply. Commercial buildings, irrigation systems and fire lines commonly have them, and they are tested on a schedule by a certified tester.

Testing requirements are set by the water provider, so ask the City of Grapevine at (817) 410-3000 which assemblies at your property need testing and how often. The plumber can test the device, repair or replace it and help you file the result with the city.

What to do first when a business floods

A few steps protect people and stock before the plumber arrives.

  • Shut off the main water valve and any equipment that uses water.
  • Keep people away from wet floors and from electrical equipment.
  • Do not use drains that are backing up.
  • Photograph the damage and note the time it started.
  • Call the 24/7 line and describe the building.
