Emergency water heater replacement in Grapevine when the tank has failed

A water heater that is leaking from the shell is finished, and the right fix is water heater replacement in Grapevine as soon as possible. Call (817) 601-6266 any hour of the day. The emergency line is open 24/7, and the plumber books the soonest appointment available. Same-day appointments are offered when the schedule allows.

Until the plumber arrives, turn off the gas or the breaker feeding the heater, then close the cold water valve above it. If there is a drain valve at the bottom and a hose, you can drain the tank to a floor drain or outside. That removes most of the water that could spill.

Replacement is more than swapping a tank. Capacity, fuel, venting, the expansion tank, the pan and drain, the shutoff valves and the permit all have to be right. Here is what a proper replacement involves and how to decide between a like-for-like swap and an upgrade.

Failure

When a failed tank is an emergency

A tank that has corroded through cannot be patched. It can release forty to fifty gallons in minutes, and the hot water can reach electrical outlets and drywall quickly.

  • Standing water around the tank with wet, rusty seams on the shell.
  • Hissing or steam from a gas unit, or a burning smell from an electric one.
  • A tank that is hot to the touch on the outside, which suggests failed insulation or an overheated vessel.
  • Any gas smell. Leave the building and call 911 or the gas utility first.

Choosing the right size and fuel

A household of two usually needs a smaller tank than a household of five with a soaking tub. The number to look at is the first-hour rating on the unit's yellow label, which tells you how much hot water it delivers in the first sixty minutes after a full heat. Matching that to your busiest hour avoids cold showers and avoids buying more tank than you need.

Staying with the same fuel is simpler and cheaper, because the gas line, vent or circuit already exists. Changing fuel means new piping, a new circuit or a new vent, and sometimes it is worth it for a different type of unit. The tankless water heater page covers that upgrade in detail.

If the old tank lasted more than ten years in Grapevine's mineral-rich water, you are getting normal service. Hardness of 117 to 135 ppm in the city's 2024 water quality report means scale, and that is one reason to flush a new tank each year.

Tank or tankless for the replacement

Both are valid. The honest answer depends on your hot water use, your space and your budget for the first install.

Tank water heater

  • Lower purchase price
  • Same fuel and venting as the old unit in most homes
  • Stores a fixed volume that can run out
  • Needs room for the tank and a drain pan

Tankless water heater

  • Heats water on demand with no stored volume
  • Wall-mounted and compact
  • Higher purchase and install cost
  • Needs descaling in hard water

Whichever you choose, ask the plumber to confirm that the venting, gas line size and electrical capacity fit the unit before any work begins.

Permits, code and the expansion tank

The City of Grapevine lists the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC), effective January 1, 2024, along with its own plumbing amendments. Its combined electrical, plumbing, mechanical and irrigation permit application includes a residential water heater change-out category, so replacements are a permitted activity category in the city's paperwork.

Permit requirements differ by the job, so ask Grapevine Building Services at (817) 410-3165 which one applies to yours. A plumber also checks that the new unit has a relief valve with a proper discharge line, and in many homes an expansion tank to absorb pressure from thermal expansion. These are safety parts, not extras.

Questions about water heater replacement in Grapevine

Do you handle water heater replacement emergencies at night or on weekends in Grapevine?

Yes. The line at (817) 601-6266 is open 24/7. A failed tank is treated as urgent, and the dispatcher books the soonest appointment available.

How long does a replacement take?

Plan on several hours for a like-for-like tank swap. Fuel changes, new venting or a tankless install take longer, and the plumber gives you the time with the price.

Can I keep the same size water heater?

Usually, yes. If the household has grown or the old unit ran out of hot water, ask the plumber to compare the first-hour rating of the old and new units before you buy.

Should I replace the unit before it fails?

If it is well past ten years old, rusting or leaking at fittings, planning the swap on your own schedule is cheaper than an emergency one. See water heater installation.

What should I do with the old heater?

Your plumber removes it. Steel tanks are scrap metal and are recycled, so you do not have to arrange disposal.

The cost of a failed water heater is not the unit, it is the water. A shell leak that runs overnight can soak flooring and drywall, and that damage costs more than the heater. The moment a tank starts leaking from the body, shut it down and call.
