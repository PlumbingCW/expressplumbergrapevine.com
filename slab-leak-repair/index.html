Emergency slab leak repair in Grapevine when water runs under the floor

A slab leak is a leak in a pipe that runs in or under the concrete slab of the house. Slab leak repair in Grapevine begins with confirming the leak and finding its location, then choosing a repair that fits the pipe and the floor. The 24/7 line at (817) 601-6266 takes calls at any hour and a plumber books the soonest appointment available. Same-day appointments are offered when the schedule allows.

If you suspect a slab leak, shut off the main water valve and see whether the meter stops. If the meter stops with the main off, the leak is inside the house or under it. Leave the main off if floors are wet or warm, and call.

Slab leaks are not rare. The hard part is that nothing shows at the surface until the damage is significant, so the signs below matter.

Signs

When a possible slab leak is an emergency

These signs mean a pressurized line is leaking under the floor and damage is already underway:

  • A warm or hot spot on a tile or concrete floor, which suggests a hot water line leaking.
  • The sound of running water when everything is off.
  • Wet carpet, buckling wood flooring or water seeping at the base of a wall.
  • A water bill that climbs without a change in how you use water.
  • Low water pressure throughout the house that has no other cause.

How a slab leak is found and fixed

The repair depends on where the leak is and how the lines run, so the first step is always detection.

Why slab leaks happen, and what is not known about Grapevine

Pipes under a slab can leak because of corrosion from the water, abrasion against gravel or rock, a shift in the slab, or a fitting that failed. Hot water lines are affected more often because heat speeds corrosion. Grapevine's 2024 water quality report lists chloramines as the disinfectant and 117 to 135 ppm hardness, but we do not claim either one causes a particular leak, because the pipe's own material and installation matter more.

We do not make claims about a specific soil type or the age of Grapevine's housing stock, because we have not found a primary source for either. A plumber can tell you what the pipe material and condition are on your own house, and that matters more than a neighborhood average.

If you are buying or selling, a pressure test before closing can save a bad surprise.

Insurance and documentation

Slab leaks are often covered by homeowner insurance for the resulting damage, but policies differ on whether the access and repair of the pipe is covered. Many cover the cost of tearing out and replacing the floor to reach the leak and not the pipe itself, while others exclude slab leaks entirely.

Document the leak with photos of the wet floor, the meter readings and the plumber's report, and contact your insurer before repairs start if you plan to claim. Ask the plumber for a written finding that names the location and the cause.

Questions about slab leak repair in Grapevine

Do you handle slab leak emergencies at night or on weekends in Grapevine?

Yes. The line at (817) 601-6266 is open 24/7. A leak that is flooding the floor is treated as urgent, and the dispatcher books the soonest appointment available.

How do I know if I have a slab leak?

A warm floor spot, the sound of running water, damp flooring and an unexplained jump in the water bill are the main signs. A meter test with the main shut off narrows it down.

Do I need to break the slab to repair it?

Not always. A reroute avoids breaking the slab by running a new line elsewhere. The plumber explains the options and the trade-offs.

Can a slab leak damage my foundation?

Water under a slab can wash out or soften soil near the foundation if it runs long enough. That is a reason to repair promptly. A structural engineer can assess any cracks.

Is a slab leak the same as a sewer leak?

No. A slab leak is in a pressurized water supply line. A sewer or drain leak is in a gravity line and is found with a camera. See sewer inspection.

A slab leak is easy to ignore and expensive to leave alone. If you suspect one, shut off the water, check the meter and call. Confirming the leak before cutting the floor is the single most useful step in the whole job.
