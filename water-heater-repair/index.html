Emergency water heater repair in Grapevine, from cold showers to a leaking tank

When the hot water stops or a tank starts leaking, you need water heater repair in Grapevine fast. The 24/7 emergency line at (817) 601-6266 takes calls at night and on weekends, and a licensed plumber can book the soonest appointment available. Same-day appointments are offered when the schedule allows.

While you wait, find the dial or switch on the heater and turn it off. For a gas unit, set the control to pilot or off. For an electric unit, switch off its breaker. Then close the cold water valve on the pipe above the tank. That stops the refill and limits a leak to the water already in the tank.

Water heater repair means diagnosing which part failed and replacing it, which is different from replacing the whole unit. A tank that is sound inside can often be repaired for far less than a new one. The sections below show which failures are repairable and which are not.

Leak alert

When a water heater leak is an emergency

Water at the base of the tank can come from a loose fitting or from a tank that has corroded through. The two have very different outcomes, so treat any puddle as urgent until someone has checked it.

  • Water pooling around the base with the tank shell wet or rusty means the tank itself has likely failed.
  • Dripping from the pipe at the side or top is often a relief valve or a fitting, and may be repairable.
  • A gas smell means leave the house and call the gas utility or 911 before calling a plumber.
  • Never open the relief valve to "test" it when the water is scalding. Steam and hot water can burn.

Common water heater failures and what they mean

Most hot water problems trace to one of a small number of parts. Knowing which makes the call to the plumber more useful.

No hot water at all

On an electric unit this is usually a tripped breaker, a failed upper element or a bad thermostat. On a gas unit it is usually a pilot, thermocouple or igniter problem, or a gas supply issue.

Hot water runs out fast

A failed lower element, a dip tube that has broken down or heavy sediment shrinks the usable hot water. Sediment from hard water is a common cause in older tanks.

Rumbling or popping noises

Water trapped under a layer of mineral scale boils and pops. Grapevine's published hardness of 117 to 135 ppm builds that scale over the years, and flushing the tank can help a young heater.

Rust-colored hot water

If only the hot side is rusty, the tank's anode rod is spent or the tank lining is failing. A new anode rod can extend a healthy tank. A corroded tank cannot be fixed.

Gas, electric and hybrid heat pump units repair differently

Gas water heaters add the burner, the pilot or igniter, the thermocouple and the venting to the list of failure points. Venting matters for safety, because a gas unit that backdrafts can put carbon monoxide into the house. A plumber checks draft and flue condition as part of any gas water heater repair.

Electric tanks have two heating elements and two thermostats, and testing each with a meter is quick. Hybrid heat pump units add a compressor, a fan and a condensate drain, and the condensate line is a frequent source of water on the floor. Hybrids also need airflow, so a unit in a closed closet may underperform.

Tankless units have their own failure list, which is covered on the tankless water heater page.

Repair or replace: the honest decision

A repair makes sense when the tank is sound and the failed part is cheap compared with the unit. It does not make sense when the tank itself is leaking, when the heater is old and has had more than one repair, or when the repair costs a large fraction of a new unit. A plumber can tell you which side of that line yours is on.

When replacement is the right call, water heater replacement covers sizing, venting and the permit question. If you are upgrading on your own schedule instead of reacting to a failure, water heater installation is the planning page.

What to do if the heater is leaking or smells of gas

Safety comes before any repair.

  • If you smell gas, leave the house and call 911 or the gas utility from outside.
  • Turn off the electric breaker or set a gas heater to off.
  • Close the cold water valve above the heater.
  • Keep children and pets away from hot water on the floor.
  • Call the 24/7 line.

Questions about water heater repair in Grapevine

Do you handle water heater emergencies at night or on weekends in Grapevine?

Yes. The line at (817) 601-6266 is open 24/7. A leaking tank or a gas smell is treated as urgent, and the dispatcher books the soonest appointment available.

How long does a water heater repair take?

Most repairs take one to two hours when the part is on the truck. A part that has to be ordered turns it into a second visit, and the plumber tells you that before work starts.

Is it safe to keep using a leaking water heater?

Shut off the heat and the cold water supply first. A slow leak at a fitting is a repair, but a tank leaking from the shell will keep leaking and can fail suddenly. Do not keep running it.

Does hard water shorten a water heater's life?

It can. The USGS notes that heating hard water forms calcium carbonate scale that shortens equipment life and cuts efficiency. Grapevine's 2024 report lists 117 ppm for the city's own water and 135 ppm for water from the Trinity River Authority. See water softener and filtration for the options.

Do I need a permit to repair or replace a water heater?

A repair of a single part usually does not, but permit rules vary. The City of Grapevine's permit application lists a residential water heater change-out category, so call Building Services at (817) 410-3165 before a replacement.

A water heater usually gives warnings before it fails: noise, rust in the hot water, a slow recovery or moisture at the base. Catching one of those early is the difference between a part replacement and a flooded garage. If you see any of them, call and have it looked at.

Codes and sources for this work

Water heater repair follows the manufacturer's instructions, and the heater's relief valve and discharge pipe are part of the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) that Grapevine adopted. If a repair becomes a replacement, Grapevine's permit application has a water heater change out category, and Building Services is at (817) 410-3165.
