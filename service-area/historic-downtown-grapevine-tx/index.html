Commercial plumbing along Historic Main Street

Historic Downtown Grapevine is a commercial district, not a residential neighborhood. The city's walking tour says Main Street is listed on the National Register of Historic Places and describes more than 50 buildings. It says at least 80 percent of the commercial buildings date from 1888, when the Cotton Belt Railroad arrived, or later.

Today the street holds shops, restaurants, galleries and offices. For the homes just off Main Street, see our page on the Original Town historic district.

Why older commercial buildings need planning

Businesses in older buildings share some concerns. Floor plans were built for earlier uses, so a kitchen, a restroom or a tasting room may sit in space that was never designed for it. Drain and supply lines can be tucked into tight spaces.

We do not know the plumbing in any particular building from the street. A plumber has to open the access points and look, and a camera inspection is often the quickest way to see a floor drain or a line under a slab.

What to do first when a business has a plumbing failure

Staff on shift should know these steps before a busy weekend.

Find the shutoffs

Post the location of the main water valve and the water heater shutoff where staff can see it.

Stop the source

Close the fixture stop valve or the main valve. Do not run drains that are backing up.

Protect people and stock

Block off wet floors and move inventory or paper goods out of the water.

Call the landlord if you rent

Some leases require the building owner to approve repairs. Check yours.

Local note

Permits and design review on Main Street

The City of Grapevine has had a Historic Preservation Ordinance since 1991. It names the Historic Township District and five local historic districts, and exterior changes in those districts and at landmarks go through a Certificate of Appropriateness from the Historic Preservation Commission.

Interior plumbing is usually separate, but permits still apply. Ask Building Services at (817) 410-3165 about the permit for your job, and ask the Historic Preservation office if anything on the outside of the building will change.

Source type: City of Grapevine Historic Preservation page, and the Grapevine Historic Downtown Walking Tour.

Questions about commercial plumbing on Main Street

Is Historic Downtown Grapevine a residential neighborhood?

No. It is the historic commercial district centered on Main Street. The nearby residential area is the Original Town Residential Historic District, which has its own page.

Do older Main Street buildings need different plumbing work?

Not by default. What matters is each building's layout and past remodels. A plumber should inspect the lines and fixtures and tell you what the building has before recommending work.

Who approves exterior changes in the historic district?

The City of Grapevine Historic Preservation Commission issues Certificates of Appropriateness for exterior changes in historic districts and at landmarks. Check with the city before work that touches the outside of a building.

Sources for this page
