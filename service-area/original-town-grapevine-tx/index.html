Plumbing in Grapevine's Original Town historic district

Original Town is the residential area just off Historic Main Street. The National Register of Historic Places lists it as the Original Town Residential Historic District, added on July 10, 1998. The listing counts 98 contributing buildings and says the district is roughly bounded by Texas, Austin, Hudgins and Jenkins Streets. It is also known as the College Heights Neighborhood.

This page is for homeowners in and around those streets. We have no claim to know what pipes are inside any one house. Old houses get remodeled, repaired and re-plumbed over the years, so the only reliable answer comes from looking at your own plumbing.

How the district is described

The Grapevine Multiple Property Submission to the National Register covers the town's historic resources, and the Original Town district is one part of it. Grapevine itself was founded in 1844, and the Cotton Belt Railroad reached town in 1888, which is when much of nearby Main Street was rebuilt in brick.

We do not publish a street list for this page because the district is documented by its bounding streets and not by a full address roll. If you are not sure whether your home sits inside the boundary, the City of Grapevine Historic Preservation office can confirm it.

What to do first when water is leaking

In an older house, knowing where the shutoffs are matters even more. Do these before you call.

Find the main shutoff

Turn the main water valve clockwise. If it will not move, do not force it. Tell the plumber.

Stay away from wet electrics

Do not touch outlets or the panel near water. Switch off a breaker only if you can reach it dry.

Note what you see

Write down where the leak is and when it started. Photos help later.

Protect the floor and walls

Move rugs and valuables and catch drips. Old plaster and wood floors react badly to standing water.

What a plumber can sort out in an older home

A house with a long history often has a mix of original and replaced plumbing. Nothing about the district's age tells us what your lines are made of. These are the jobs that come up in any older house, and they are worth asking about:

  • A slow drain that keeps coming back, which a camera can trace. See drain cleaning.
  • A water heater of unknown age, where the data plate shows when it was made. See water heater replacement.
  • A high water bill with no obvious cause, which starts with a meter test. See leak detection.
  • Supply lines that have been patched many times, where a repipe estimate may be worth a look.

Ask the plumber to show you what is in the wall or crawlspace before agreeing to a bigger job.

Local note

Check with the city before exterior work

The City of Grapevine has had a Historic Preservation Ordinance since 1991. It lists the Historic Township District and five local historic districts, plus individual landmarks. Exterior changes in those districts and at landmarks go through design review with a Certificate of Appropriateness from the Historic Preservation Commission.

Most plumbing is inside the walls, but a vent, a relocated water heater enclosure or a new meter box can touch the outside of a house. Ask the Historic Preservation office, or Building Services at (817) 410-3165 about permits, before work begins.

Source type: City of Grapevine Historic Preservation page, and the National Register listing for the Original Town Residential Historic District (NRIS 98000736).

Questions about plumbing in Original Town

Is every home in Original Town covered by the historic district?

No. The National Register district is roughly bounded by Texas, Austin, Hudgins and Jenkins Streets, so homes outside those streets may not be in it. The City of Grapevine Historic Preservation office can tell you whether your address falls under local design review.

Do I need approval to replace a water heater in a historic home?

Permit rules depend on the job. The City of Grapevine lists a residential water heater change-out on its combined plumbing permit application, so call Building Services at (817) 410-3165. Ask the Historic Preservation office too if anything outside the house will change.

Are older homes in Grapevine full of galvanized or cast iron pipe?

There is no way to say that for a whole district. Pipe material depends on the individual house and what has been replaced. A plumber can inspect yours, and a camera inspection shows the inside of a drain line without digging.
