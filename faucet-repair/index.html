Emergency faucet repair in Grapevine for drips, leaks and valves that will not close

Faucet repair in Grapevine covers kitchen and bathroom faucets, shower valves, outdoor spigots and the shutoff valves beneath your sinks. If water will not stop, find the shutoff valve under the sink and turn it clockwise. If that valve will not close, shut off the main water valve. The 24/7 line at (817) 601-6266 takes calls any hour, and a plumber books the soonest appointment available.

Same-day appointments are offered when the schedule allows. A dripping faucet by itself can wait for the next day.

The part that fails depends on the faucet type. Knowing which type you have makes the repair faster.

Valve failure

When a faucet or valve problem is an emergency

Most drips are a daytime fix. These are not:

  • Water that will not stop when the faucet is off and the supply stop will not close.
  • A shower valve that leaks inside the wall, with water stains on the other side.
  • A broken supply line or stop valve spraying water.
  • An outdoor spigot that has split after a freeze and is leaking into the wall.

Faucet types and how each fails

Four basic designs account for most faucets.

Compression

Rubber washers wear, and the faucet drips when closed. Replacing the washer and seat fixes it.

Cartridge

A cartridge controls flow and temperature, and O-rings wear. A new cartridge usually fixes a leak or a stiff handle.

Ball

Springs and seals wear in the ball valve, causing leaks at the base. A repair kit replaces them.

Ceramic disc

Discs last long but can scale up in hard water. Cleaning or a new cartridge restores them.

Hard water and faucets

Grapevine's 2024 water quality report lists 117 to 135 ppm hardness. Minerals collect in aerators and on cartridges, and they cause low flow, sputtering and stiff handles. Soaking aerators in vinegar often fixes the flow.

If you see scale on fixtures, the cause is the water and not the faucet. See water softener and filtration for the options.

Shutoff valves and shower valves

The small valves under a sink are called angle stops. They are the quickest way to shut water off to one fixture, and they seize with age if they are never turned. An old stop that will not close or that leaks at the stem should be replaced, and the plumber does that during a faucet visit.

A shower valve is behind the wall, so a leak at the handle or the showerhead may be a worn cartridge, a loose connection or a failed valve body. If the valve is leaking inside the wall, call right away, because water can run unseen. A leak detection visit finds out where it goes.

Questions about faucet repair in Grapevine

Do you handle faucet emergencies at night or on weekends in Grapevine?

Yes. The line at (817) 601-6266 is open 24/7. A faucet or valve that will not shut off is treated as urgent, and the dispatcher books the soonest appointment available.

How much water does a dripping faucet waste?

A faucet that drips steadily can waste thousands of gallons a year. The EPA reports about 9,400 gallons a year for the average household's total leaks.

Why is my faucet still dripping after I replaced the washer?

The valve seat may be worn or scaled. A new seat or a cartridge replacement is the next step.

Can you repair a shower valve?

Yes. Most shower valve repairs are a new cartridge, though a failed valve body may need access through the wall.

Should I replace the faucet or repair it?

If it is relatively new and the part is available, repair. If it is old, corroded or special order, replacement is often better value.

A faucet repair is one of the cheapest plumbing jobs and one of the easiest to put off. A small drip becomes a stained sink, and a stuck shutoff valve is the one you need when something else fails.
