Sewer camera inspection in Grapevine, so the repair is based on what the pipe looks like

A sewer camera inspection puts a waterproof camera on a flexible rod and runs it through the line from a cleanout, recording what it finds. A sewer inspection in Grapevine shows roots, cracks, offsets, bellies, grease and scale with a locator that marks where on the surface the problem sits. The line at (817) 601-6266 takes planning calls, and same-day appointments are offered when the schedule allows.

An inspection is the right first step when a line keeps backing up, when you are about to buy a house or when a plumber wants to quote a repair. It is a planned job, not an emergency one. If sewage is backing up right now, read emergency sewer line repair.

Here is what the camera finds and how to read what you are shown.

What a sewer camera can show

The video tells you whether the pipe is clean, damaged or worn out.

Roots

Fine roots enter at joints and grow into hairlike masses. A cutting tool clears them but the root path remains.

Cracks and breaks

A hairline crack or a broken section lets water out and soil in.

Offset joints

Pipes that have shifted leave a lip that catches waste.

A belly

A low spot holds water and solids, and the camera shows standing water in the pipe.

Buildup

Grease, scale and mineral coating narrow the pipe.

Material

The camera shows whether the pipe is clay, cast iron, PVC or another material.

How an inspection works

A full inspection takes about an hour for a typical residential line.

1

Access

The plumber finds a cleanout or removes a toilet to reach the line.

2

3

A locator marks the location and depth of any problem above ground.

4

You see the video and get an explanation and a written finding.

Inspections before buying a house

A home inspector looks at what is visible. A sewer line is buried, and most home inspections do not include a camera. Buyers in Grapevine and Southlake can ask the seller for permission to run a camera before closing, and the report can be used to negotiate a repair or a price change.

A camera inspection records the condition of the pipe on the day you looked. It is not a guarantee of how long the pipe will last, but it tells you whether you are buying a line with obvious damage.

Reading the report

Ask the plumber to show you the video at the problem spot, not just a summary. Look for the footage marker that shows how far from the cleanout the problem is, and the locator mark on the ground. The finding should name the problem, give the location and describe the repair options.

If the report finds a problem, sewer line repair covers lining, pipe bursting and replacement. If the pipe is sound but coated, hydro jetting cleans it. If it is simply blocked, sewer line cleaning opens it.

Questions about sewer inspection in Grapevine

When should I get a sewer camera inspection?

Get one when a line has backed up more than once, before buying a home or before any repair is quoted.

Does the inspection fix anything?

No. It shows the problem. Cleaning and repair are separate steps, quoted from what the camera shows.

Will you give me the video?

Ask for a recording and a written finding. They are useful for negotiating a purchase and for insurance.

Do you do inspections as emergencies?

A camera is often used during an emergency backup after the line is opened. The 24/7 line at (817) 601-6266 takes those calls.

How accurate is the locator?

The locator marks the camera's position and depth from above ground, usually within a small area. The plumber confirms it before digging.

A camera inspection turns a guess into a decision. It can also tell you that the line is fine, which is worth knowing too.
