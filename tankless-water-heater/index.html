Tankless water heater installation, repair and flushing in Grapevine

A tankless water heater heats water only when a tap is open, so there is no tank to run out or rust through. Tankless water heater service in Grapevine covers new installs, repairs and the yearly descaling that keeps the heat exchanger working. The line at (817) 601-6266 is open 24/7, and same-day appointments are offered when the schedule allows.

Tankless is not the right choice for every home. It costs more to install, needs the gas line and vent to be sized for it and can struggle if several fixtures run at once. It is a strong choice when you have limited space, a big household or a tank that keeps running out.

The biggest long-term variable is water quality. Grapevine's published numbers explain why descaling is not optional here.

Why Grapevine's water makes descaling matter

The City of Grapevine's 2024 water quality report lists total hardness of 117 ppm for water it treats from Lake Grapevine and 135 ppm for water it buys from the Trinity River Authority. USGS classes 61 to 120 mg/L as moderately hard and 121 to 180 as hard, so Grapevine's supply sits across that boundary.

When hard water is heated, calcium carbonate comes out as scale. A tank can hide it as sediment in the bottom. A tankless unit cannot, because the water passes through narrow channels in the heat exchanger and scale reduces flow and heat transfer there. USGS notes that scale can shorten equipment life and raise heating costs.

Descaling a tankless water heater

Descaling is a flush. The plumber isolates the unit with its service valves, circulates a mild acid solution through the heat exchanger with a small pump, then rinses and restores the valves. It takes about an hour and removes the scale that would otherwise choke the unit.

Many manufacturers recommend flushing about once a year in hard water. Your unit's manual sets the real interval, and some units have error codes that point to scale. A water softener upstream reduces what there is to flush, as covered in water softener and filtration. If your unit has never been flushed, there is a good chance it needs one now.

Common tankless problems

Most issues trace to scale, a supply problem or a sensor.

Lukewarm water or temperature swings

Scale in the heat exchanger or a clogged inlet screen reduces heat transfer. A flush and a screen cleaning often fix it.

Error codes on the display

Codes point to ignition, flame, venting or flow faults. The code and the unit's manual narrow the cause quickly.

Water takes a long time to turn hot

This is partly normal, since the unit waits for flow. A recirculation setup or a point-of-use fix can help.

Cold water pockets between uses

Some units cannot fire at very low flow. Adjusting the faucet or the unit's settings may help.

Tankless compared with a tank unit

The decision is about hot water habits and space.

Traditional tank

  • Lower purchase cost
  • Simple install on existing venting
  • Hot water can run out
  • Occupies floor space

Tankless unit

  • Heats on demand and does not run out in a single draw
  • Compact wall mount
  • Higher purchase and install cost
  • Needs yearly descaling in hard water

If you are weighing the two, the water heater installation page covers sizing and the planning steps.

Gas line, venting and electrical needs

A tankless gas unit draws a large volume of gas when it fires, often more than the tank it replaces, so the gas line must be sized for the load. Venting is usually different too, sometimes through the wall with sealed pipe. An electric tankless unit draws a heavy current and needs a dedicated circuit, and many homes need a panel upgrade to support one.

Ask the plumber to confirm gas line size, vent routing and electrical capacity before ordering a unit. The City of Grapevine lists the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) with its amendments, and Building Services at (817) 410-3165 can tell you which permit applies to the work.

Questions about tankless water heater in Grapevine

Does a tankless water heater have an anode rod?

Most tankless units do not, because there is no steel tank to protect from corrosion. The part that needs care is the heat exchanger, which is why scale control matters.

How often should a tankless water heater be flushed in Grapevine?

Many manufacturers recommend about once a year in hard water. Your unit's manual and its error history set the real interval. Grapevine's published hardness is 117 to 135 ppm.

Can a tankless unit replace my tank without new pipes?

Sometimes. The gas line size, venting and electrical capacity decide it. The plumber checks them before quoting.

Do you repair tankless units at night or on weekends?

Yes. The line at (817) 601-6266 is open 24/7, and the dispatcher books the soonest appointment available.

Is a water softener worth it with a tankless heater?

It reduces scale and the flush frequency. It does not remove the need for a flush, and it adds its own maintenance, covered on the water softener page.

Tankless water heaters have a long service life when they are cared for, and they are one of the few appliances where a yearly hour of maintenance changes the outcome. In Grapevine, that hour is a flush.
