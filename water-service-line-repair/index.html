Emergency water service line repair in Grapevine for leaks between the meter and the house

The water service line runs underground from the city meter to the house and carries every drop you use. Water service line repair in Grapevine fixes a leak or a failed section of that pipe. The 24/7 line at (817) 601-6266 takes calls any hour, and a plumber books the soonest appointment available. Same-day appointments are offered when the schedule allows.

If water is surfacing in the yard or pooling at the curb, shut off the main valve at the house and see whether the meter keeps moving. If it does, the leak is between the meter and the house. Leave the water off and call.

This page covers how the line fails, how a leak is found and the repair choices.

Buried leak

When a service line leak is an emergency

A buried leak can run quietly for weeks, but these signs mean it is already serious:

  • Water surfacing in the yard, driveway or at the curb.
  • A sudden drop in water pressure throughout the house.
  • A meter that keeps turning with the main valve at the house shut off.
  • A sinkhole, a soft patch or an unusually green strip of lawn over the line.
  • A water bill much higher than normal with no change in use.

How a service line leak is found and fixed

The goal is to find the leak without digging up the whole yard.

A meter test with the house valve off shows the leak is on the buried line.

Low water pressure and the service line

A leak or a partly collapsed service line can cut the pressure the house gets. A failing pressure regulator, a closed valve or scale-clogged fixtures also cause low pressure, so a plumber checks those before digging. A pressure gauge on a hose bib shows what the house is getting.

High pressure is also a problem, because it stresses pipes and fittings. A pressure reducing valve near the main controls it. If you see sudden changes, ask a plumber to test the pressure.

Who owns what, and who to call

The main in the street and the meter belong to the water system, and the line from there to the house is the part that serves your property. Where the city's responsibility ends decides who pays, so ask the City of Grapevine at (817) 410-3000 before you pay for a repair near the meter.

Digging in a yard can hit buried utilities. Call 811, the free utility location service, before any excavation. A plumber who is doing the work will normally arrange the locate. If the line crosses under a sidewalk or street, the city may need to be involved, and Building Services at (817) 410-3165 can say whether a permit applies.

Questions about water service line repair in Grapevine

Do you handle service line emergencies at night or on weekends in Grapevine?

Yes. The line at (817) 601-6266 is open 24/7. A leak that is flooding the yard is treated as urgent, and the dispatcher books the soonest appointment available.

How do I know if my service line is leaking?

A meter that moves with the house valve off, a soft spot in the yard and a pressure drop are the main signs.

Who is responsible for the line between the meter and the house?

That depends on where the city's ownership ends. Ask the City of Grapevine at (817) 410-3000 before paying for a repair near the meter.

Can the line be replaced without digging up the yard?

Sometimes. Trenchless methods need entry and exit pits and suitable conditions. The plumber explains whether your line qualifies.

What should I do before digging?

Call 811 for a free utility locate. Do not dig before the locate is done.
