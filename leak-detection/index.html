When a hidden leak is an emergency

Some leaks can wait for a daytime appointment. These cannot:

  • Water dripping from a ceiling, especially near light fixtures or a ceiling fan.
  • A wall or floor that is wet to the touch or has started to bubble or buckle.
  • A sudden spike in the water meter reading with no one home or running water.
  • The sound of running water from inside a wall when every fixture is off.
  • If water is near outlets, turn off power to that area from the panel only if you can do so safely, and keep people away.

How a leak is found

A good leak detection visit is a process of elimination. It starts with the cheapest test and moves to the more specific one.

1

Meter test

With all fixtures off, the plumber watches the meter to confirm a leak exists.

2

Pressure test

Isolating sections of the supply lines shows which section is losing pressure.

3

Listen and scan

Acoustic tools and thermal cameras pinpoint the spot within a small area.

4

Confirm and quote

The leak is confirmed before any opening is made, and the repair gets a clear price.

Where hidden leaks usually are

The location tells you what tool is needed and what repair is likely.

Behind a wall or above a ceiling

Supply and drain lines in a wall can leak at joints, valves and fittings. Thermal imaging and moisture meters show wet areas, and acoustic tools pick up the sound of a leak.

Under the slab

A leak in a line under concrete is hard to reach. Hot spots, a running-water sound and a rising bill are signs, and the slab leak repair page covers it.

In the yard or the service line

A wet patch of lawn, a low-pressure complaint and a moving meter outside the house point to the buried line. See water service line repair.

At a fixture

A running toilet, a dripping faucet or a failing supply stop can look like a house leak on the meter. A dye test and a quick look find these first.

The high water bill leak test you can do first

A high water bill does not always mean a hidden leak. A toilet flapper that does not seal can waste hundreds of gallons a day and is the most common cause. Drop a few drops of food coloring in the tank, wait fifteen minutes without flushing and look for color in the bowl. If it shows, the flapper is the leak.

If every fixture is off and the meter still turns, the leak is between the meter and a fixture. Shut off the main valve at the house. If the meter stops, the leak is inside the house. If it keeps turning, the leak is on the buried line between the meter and the house.

Irrigation systems, pool fill lines and outdoor spigots are also frequent sources, so check them before you call. A plumber can pressure-test the system to be sure.

Leaks and water bills in Grapevine and Southlake

The City of Grapevine publishes its annual water quality report and told residents with unusual bills after the February 2021 storm to contact utility billing. If you see an unexplained spike, start with City Hall at (817) 410-3000 and ask for utility billing about your meter reading and the account history.

In nearby Southlake the city allows a leak adjustment on a residential water bill once in a rolling twelve-month period, and it requires documentation such as plumber invoices within ninety days of the leak. Pool fills are not eligible. Keep your leak detection invoice if you may need to apply.

During Winter Storm Uri in February 2021, Grapevine's own water use reached about 20 million gallons on February 16, against a normal winter day near 5 million. A leak you find early is a small repair, and a leak you find during a citywide event is a bigger one.

Questions about leak detection in Grapevine

Do you handle leak emergencies at night or on weekends in Grapevine?

Yes. The line at (817) 601-6266 is open 24/7. Active leaks are treated as urgent, and the dispatcher books the soonest appointment available.

How do I know if I have a hidden leak?

Warning signs are a high water bill, a meter that moves with everything off, damp or discolored drywall, a musty smell and the sound of running water. Any one of them is worth a meter check.

Do I have to break open walls to find the leak?

Usually not. Acoustic tools, thermal imaging and pressure tests narrow the location so only a small opening is needed to reach the pipe.

Can a plumber test my water bill spike?

Yes. The first step is a meter test with all fixtures off, followed by toilets, irrigation and the service line.

What if the leak is in the sewer line?

Drain and sewer leaks are found with a camera, not a water pressure test. See sewer line camera inspection.

Should I worry about mold?

Water that stays in drywall or insulation can grow mold in days to weeks. Dry the area and fix the source quickly, then have the area checked if you see staining or smell mustiness.

A small leak is cheap to fix if you find it before it becomes damage. The meter, the dye test and a few minutes of listening are free, and they tell you whether the problem is inside the house, under it or outside it.

If you have run the tests and the leak is still unexplained, call and let a plumber trace it.
