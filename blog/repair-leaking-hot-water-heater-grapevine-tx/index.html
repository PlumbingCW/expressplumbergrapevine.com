Where the water comes from decides whether it is a ten-minute fix or a dead tank. Find the source before you touch anything.

First steps when a water heater is leaking

A leaking water heater needs quick action, but not panic. The first goal is to limit damage, and the second is to find where the water comes from. Some leaks are loose fittings that cost little to fix. A leak from the tank itself means the heater has failed.

Turn off the power at the breaker or set a gas unit to pilot or off. Close the cold water supply valve on top of the heater. If water is spreading, shut off the main valve for the house. Then look closely, with a flashlight, before you decide what to do.

The sections below walk through the common leak points and what each one means.

Where the water is coming from

Trace the leak from the top down. Water runs downhill, so the source is higher than the puddle.

Leak pointLikely causeFixable?
Pipe connections on topLoose or corroded fittingUsually, by tightening or replacing the fitting
Temperature and pressure relief valveValve is worn, or pressure or temperature is too highReplace the valve, then find the cause
Drain valve at the bottomWorn valve or washerOften, with a new valve
Heating element gasket (electric)Gasket or element has failedOften, with a new gasket or element
Tank itself, or water pooling beneathCorrosion has breached the tankNo. Replace the heater

Leaks that sound serious but are not always

Two things are often mistaken for a failed tank.

Condensation

A cold-water pipe or a high-efficiency gas unit can sweat and drip. Wipe it dry and see whether it comes back.

Relief valve discharge pipe dripping

A small drip can be a pressure issue, such as a thermal expansion problem. See our guide to water heater repair for what a plumber checks.

What a plumber checks when a heater leaks

The order matters, because the cheap causes come first.

1

Confirm the source

Dry every surface, then watch for the first drop.

2

Check fittings and valves

Tighten or replace corroded connections, the drain valve and the relief valve.

3

Test pressure

Water pressure above the heater's rating stresses the tank and the valve.

4

Check for an expansion tank

A closed system without one can push water out of the relief valve.

5

Inspect the tank

Rust trails, wet insulation or a leak under the jacket mean the tank has failed.

When a leaking heater should be replaced

If the tank is leaking, no sealant or patch will hold, and a heater that has leaked is likely near the end of its life. Replacement is the right call. For how that job runs, see water heater replacement.

If the leak is a fitting, a valve or an element gasket, repair is worth it, especially if the heater is relatively new.

Do not ignore a small leak

A heater that weeps slowly can rot the floor, grow mold in the closet or fail all at once. A drain pan with a pipe to a safe drain catches small leaks and gives you time. Tell your plumber if the heater sits above a finished room, because the pan and drain line matter more there.

Safety points while you wait

A few rules keep the problem from getting worse.

Do not touch wet electrical parts

Turn off the breaker only if you can reach it without standing in water.

Gas smell means leave

If you smell gas, leave the house and call 911 or the gas utility from outside.

Hot water scalds

The water that leaks can be very hot. Keep children and pets back.

Take pictures

Photograph the heater, the label and the damage for your insurer.

Electric and gas heaters leak differently

An electric heater can leak at an element gasket, and the water may run down the outside of the tank. Switch off the breaker before you touch it. A gas heater can leak at the same valves and fittings, and it also has a burner compartment where water can put out the pilot and create a hazard.

If water has reached the burner area or the control valve of a gas heater, do not relight it. A plumber should inspect it first. Water in the wrong place can damage the gas control, and a new control may be needed.

Cleanup and insurance

Once the leak is stopped, remove standing water and dry the area fast. Fans and a dehumidifier help. Wet drywall and insulation can grow mold if left for days. Take photos before you clean, and keep the plumber's invoice and the old part if you can. Many policies cover sudden water damage but not the heater itself, so read yours.

Local note

Permits, plumbers and the 2021 storm in Grapevine

Water heater work follows the 2021 International Plumbing Code (IPC) that the city adopted. The City of Grapevine's combined permit application includes a category for a residential A/C and water heater change out. That means replacing a heater is normally a permit item, so ask your plumber who pulls it. Grapevine Building Services can be reached at (817) 410-3165.

After the February 2021 winter storm, the city waived permit fees for plumbing repairs. Fees may otherwise apply, so ask before you assume a repair is exempt. You can also verify any plumber's license on the Texas State Board of Plumbing Examiners Public License Search before you hire.

Questions homeowners ask

Is a leaking water heater an emergency?

A leak from the tank is urgent. Shut off the power or gas and the water supply and call. A drip from a fitting is less urgent but should not wait long.

Does Grapevine waive permit fees for plumbing repairs?

The city waived them after the February 2021 winter storm. Fees may otherwise apply, so ask Building Services at (817) 410-3165.

Can a leaking tank be patched?

No. A tank that leaks has corroded through and needs to be replaced.

Why is my relief valve dripping?

It may be worn, or the water pressure or temperature is too high. A plumber can test pressure and check for an expansion tank.

Should I put a pan under my heater?

Yes, especially if the heater sits above living space. A pan with a drain line catches small leaks.

Finding the source of the leak is half the repair. Fittings and valves are cheap to fix, and a leaking tank is a replacement. Either way, act quickly.
