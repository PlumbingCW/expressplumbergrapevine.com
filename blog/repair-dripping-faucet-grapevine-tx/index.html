How to Repair a Dripping Faucet in Grapevine, TX

A drip means a worn part, not a broken faucet. Match the fix to the faucet type and most drips stop in under an hour.

Why a faucet drips and how to stop it

Almost every dripping faucet has one worn part inside the valve. Water pressure pushes past a hardened washer, a cracked O-ring or a scaled cartridge, and the faucet cannot close fully. The fix depends on how your faucet is built.

A steady drip adds up. The EPA reports that the average household loses about 9,400 gallons of water a year to leaks of all kinds. A single faucet is only part of that total, but it is the easiest part to fix.

Read the four faucet types below, find yours and follow the steps. If the faucet is old or corroded, replacing it may be the better value.

Find your faucet type and the part that wears

Look at the handle. How it moves points to the design.

TypeHow to spot itUsual worn part
CompressionTwo handles that you turn several timesRubber washer and seat
CartridgeOne or two handles that move smoothlyCartridge or its O-rings
BallA single lever over a rounded capSprings and seals in the ball assembly
Ceramic discA single lever with a cylindrical bodyDiscs or seals, often scale

Basic repair steps for any faucet

Always start with the water off.

1

Shut off the supply

Turn the valves under the sink clockwise. Open the faucet to release pressure.

2

Plug the drain

Block the drain with a rag so small parts cannot fall in.

3

Remove the handle

Pop off the cap, remove the screw and lift the handle.

4

Pull the part

Remove the retaining nut and the cartridge, stem or ball.

5

Match and replace

Take the old part to a supply store and buy an exact match. Replace the O-rings at the same time.

6

Reassemble and test

Put it back in reverse order, open the supply slowly and check for drips.

If the drip returns

A drip that comes back points to a deeper cause.

A worn valve seat

On compression faucets, a pitted seat chews up new washers. It can be resurfaced or replaced.

Scale and minerals

Mineral buildup keeps the part from sealing. Soaking parts in vinegar can clear light scale.

High water pressure

Pressure that is too high wears seals fast. A plumber can test it.

A cracked body

Corrosion can crack the faucet. Replace it.

Repair or replace the faucet

If the faucet is relatively new and parts are available, repair it. If it is old, corroded or no longer made, replacement is often the better value, and it is a chance to update the look. Our faucet repair page covers what a plumber checks.

Shutoff valves under the sink

If the little valves under the sink will not turn or leak when you try, stop. Forcing them can break the stem and flood the cabinet. A plumber can replace them, and that is the best time to do it, before an emergency makes you need them.

Tools and parts to gather first

You need an adjustable wrench, a screwdriver set, a hex key set, pliers, plumber's grease, a small container for screws and a rag to protect the finish. Take the old part with you to the store, because brands and sizes differ even when the faucets look alike.

Many faucets have a model number on the body or inside the cabinet. A repair kit for that exact model usually includes every seal you need and saves a second trip.

Low flow and sputtering are a different problem

If water comes out weakly or sprays sideways, the aerator at the spout tip is probably clogged with scale or debris. Unscrew it, rinse it and soak it in vinegar. If both hot and cold are weak at every faucet, the cause is elsewhere in the house, and our water service line repair page explains pressure problems.

A dripping outdoor spigot

Outdoor hose bibs drip for the same reasons, a worn washer or packing. If water leaks from behind the handle, tighten the packing nut a quarter turn. If water leaks inside the wall, shut the supply and call a plumber, because a hidden leak can rot framing. Disconnect hoses when not in use.

Kitchen, bathroom and tub faucets differ slightly

Kitchen faucets often have a pull-down sprayer, and a leak under the sink can come from the hose connection, not the spout. Bathroom faucets are usually simpler. Tub and shower valves sit inside the wall, so shut off the water to the house before you open one, and expect to replace the cartridge rather than a washer.

If the handle is stiff or the water temperature drifts, the cartridge is worn. Replace it with an exact match, grease the O-rings lightly and keep the old part for comparison. A shower valve that leaks inside the wall is a job for a plumber.

Local note

Hard water and faucets in Grapevine

According to Grapevine's 2024 water quality report, hardness is 117 ppm for city water and 135 ppm for water from the Trinity River Authority. Minerals gather on aerators, cartridges and discs, and they are a common reason a repaired faucet starts to drip or sputter again.

If you see white crust at the spout, soak the aerator in vinegar and clean the cartridge. If scale returns quickly, the cause is the water. For water quality questions the city lists 817.410.3330 for its water quality contact.

Questions homeowners ask

How much water does a dripping faucet waste?

It depends on the drip rate. The EPA reports that the average household loses about 9,400 gallons a year to leaks overall.

Why does my faucet drip after I changed the washer?

The valve seat may be worn or scaled. Resurface or replace it, or switch to a new cartridge.

Does Grapevine's hard water cause leaky faucets?

It can contribute. Grapevine's report lists 117 to 135 ppm hardness, and scale keeps parts from sealing.

Do I need to shut off the main water to fix a faucet?

No. The small valves under the sink are enough. If they will not close, shut off the main.

When should I call a plumber?

Call if the valves will not close, the faucet body is cracked or the leak is inside a wall.

Most dripping faucets are cheap to fix. Find the type, replace the worn part and test. If the part is fine and the drip stays, look at the seat, the water and the shutoffs.
